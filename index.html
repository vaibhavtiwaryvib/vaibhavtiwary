About

From flies to the clinic: the biology of healthy aging

I am a postdoctoral researcher at the Hamburg Center for Kidney Health, University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf (UKE). My work sits at the intersection of metabolism, aging biology and kidney physiology, and the fruit fly is my model throughout.

During my PhD in the lab of Shahaf Peleg at the Research Institute for Farm Animal Biology (FBN) and the University of Rostock, I used optogenetic tools to reprogram mitochondrial energy production in Drosophila melanogaster and studied pharmacological interventions that extend lifespan. My master's thesis explored how heat-stress experiences can be passed on to the next generations through the male germline.

● Currently imaging

Postdoctoral Researcher in the lab of Dr. Sybille Köhler (Hamburg Center for Kidney Health, III. Department of Medicine, UKE). I study Drosophila nephrocytes, the fly counterpart of mammalian kidney podocytes, with a focus on the slit diaphragm, the specialised cell junction that acts as the filtration barrier. I am also modelling autoimmune podocytopathy in the fly.

Research

Research themes

Using Drosophila as a model, I combine optogenetics, pharmacology, imaging and genomics to ask how cellular energy, epigenetic state and organ function shape lifespan and stress resilience.

CH1 · POSTDOC · CURRENT

Nephrocytes and the slit diaphragm

Using fly nephrocytes to understand how the kidney's filtration barrier is formed and maintained, to model autoimmune podocytopathy, and to see what happens to the barrier in disease and aging.

More about this project
The question
How do nephrocytes form and maintain their slit diaphragm, and what happens when the immune system turns against it, as in autoimmune podocytopathy?
Why the fly
Nephrocytes share the core slit diaphragm proteins with human podocytes, and fly genetics makes it fast to test many genes and conditions.
Methods
Nephrocyte dissection, confocal imaging and electron microscopy sample preparation.

CH2 · PHD

Optogenetic reprogramming of mitochondria

Light-activated proton pumps that partially take over ATP production, bypassing the electron transport chain to test how mitochondrial energetics drive aging.

More about this project
The question
Can light-driven proton pumping take over part of the mitochondria's energy production, and what does that do to aging?
Approach
Mitochondria-targeted, light-activated proton pumps in flies, custom LED setups and ATP assays.
Read more
Using light to drive energy transduction in metazoan aging, Trends in Biochemical Sciences (2023)

CH3 · PHD

Pharmacology of aging

Reserpine-induced monoamine depletion extends lifespan in Drosophila, but at a cost to heat-stress resilience.

More about this project
The finding
Reserpine, which depletes monoamines, extends lifespan in flies, but the same flies cope worse with heat stress. Longer life and robustness don't always go together.
Approach
Lifespan and heat-stress assays in Drosophila.
Read more
Reserpine prolongs lifespan but compromises heat-stress resilience, npj Aging (2026)

CH4 · MASTER'S THESIS

Transgenerational epigenetic inheritance

How heat-stress memory is transmitted through the male germline, with a focus on Polycomb/Trithorax group proteins.

More about this project
The question
Can a heat-stress experience in fathers leave a molecular memory in their offspring?
Focus
The male germline and Polycomb/Trithorax group proteins, which help keep genes switched on or off across cell divisions.

Beyond the lab

How I think, in and out of the lab

I'm very good at turning a minor everyday inconvenience into a small reverse-engineering project, especially when I suspect the official explanation is incomplete.

"But why?"

Knowing that something works is never quite enough. I want the mechanism, whether it's a mitochondrial proton pump or the chemistry inside a self-heating patch.

Is that limit real?

When two devices share the same hardware but only one gets a feature, I want to know if it's physics or a software switch. I like finding the line between impossible, not allowed and not offered by default.

Everything is an experiment

A washing machine that won't start, or a bed frame missing the right support, gets the same treatment as a lifespan assay. I find the variable responsible and change it.

One step further

If it's possible, how? If we know how, has anyone tested it? If it's been tested, how convincing is the evidence? Explanations that stop one step too early bother me.

observation → inconsistency → mechanism → evidence → workaround

A small contradiction I'm happy to admit: I'll obsess over the precise behaviour of a single protein or gadget, but I'd rather not plan my week in a calendar. Precision in understanding matters more to me than rigid structure.
